# Concept: The Blade at the Threshold

> [!info] Concept file
> Status: `exploring` (being developed), `chosen` (the DM decided; ready to integrate), `integrated` (applied to the campaign files), or `discarded` (kept for the record). Nothing here is campaign canon until it is integrated.

## Idea

**Working recommendation:** Caspian begins with a clear target: the werewolves who destroyed Ashcairn. He may also be leaping from "they claimed to serve Strahd" to "Strahd ordered the raid." The campaign can test that assumption without shaming or correcting the player out of character: evidence will show that the pack ranged beyond the Mists independently during Strahd's long slumber, and the attack predated his awakening. Strahd remains responsible for the world and powers he commands, but may not have ordered this specific atrocity.

As that answer develops, Caspian learns that the raid was one part of a wider history of people crossing between worlds, while Strahd's Barovia is itself bound to the Shadowfell. His blade remains a tool for confronting harm, but the deeper question becomes what he chooses to protect once revenge no longer explains every danger in front of him. His arc can culminate in helping the party resist Strahd's attempt to manipulate the Mists, without making Caspian the sole key to the campaign's climax.

This builds from the existing backstory and planning: Ashcairn stands on a Shadowfell-touched fold; the raid happened before Strahd's awakening, while the pack ranged beyond Barovia on its own; and the Vistani brought Caspian into the Mists. The raid remains personal, but neither its origin nor Caspian's importance has to wait until the den.

### What the session logs show

- **Session 2:** Caspian notices a shadow crossing the window. He later takes the orb at the Darklord shrine. No logged statement connects either action to his backstory.
- **Session 3:** During the Death House sacrifice discussion, Caspian is recorded as having no opinion and wanting to escape, regardless of the method. Afterward he tells Grim more about the werewolf attack and expresses hatred of Strahd.
- **Session 4:** Caspian explains to the party how much he hates Strahd. The log does not record him pursuing Strahd or a werewolf lead as a separate action.
- **Session 6:** Grim asks whether he is set on facing Strahd. The log records the question, not Caspian's answer.
- **Session 7:** Strahd greets the party at the crossroads; Caspian's reaction is not recorded.

The backstory itself says the survivors reported that the attackers spoke of serving "Devil Strahd." So Caspian's suspicion and hatred have an in-character basis. The logs support a quick emotional focus on Strahd, but do **not** establish that he believes Strahd personally ordered the Ashcairn raid, or prove metagaming. Ask the player what Caspian believes; then let later evidence distinguish Strahd's broad responsibility from direct command of this particular raid.

## Arc beats

1. **The named enemy (Arc C, now).** Session 7 ends with Strahd greeting the party at the River Ivlis Crossroads. At Tser Pool and during the werewolf hunt, Caspian can test the beliefs he brought from Ashcairn: who actually commands these hunters, and what does "serving Strahd" mean? Give him a tangible clue tied to the raid, but not Ravka's full identity or the den's location. A brief shadow-boundary sensation can recur from the window-shadow he noticed in Death House (Session 2), without explanation or mechanical reward.
2. **The trail becomes a question (Act II).** Use existing werewolf encounters and rumors around Lake Baratok to establish that the pack's behavior changed after Strahd woke, while Ashcairn was attacked before that. Caspian can investigate the contradiction and decide how much risk to take for more evidence. The clues should come from at least two sources, so one missed scene does not stall his story.
3. **A blade's judgment (Act II-III).** Encounters with cursed or coerced people create chances to distinguish an aggressor from someone being used by a greater power. Caspian is not assigned Ivan's Wolfir lore, Grim's family-rescue story, Agni's cure quest, or Elga's work with souls. He may act alongside those characters, but his question is what justice requires when the target is not the whole cause.
4. **The Ashcairn truth (Arc L, if retained).** At the den, Caspian can identify Ravka as the raid leader and learn what became of the children taken from Ashcairn. By then the first full moon has already passed, so any children still there are not a pre-transformation ticking clock. If they are alive, they are people with their own responses to Caspian; identifying, protecting, and earning their trust matters more than a rushed cure deadline. Caspian chooses what justice means for Ravka and the den rather than having the answer dictated by Ivan's family needs.
5. **Consequences, not a second delayed reveal (late Act III).** The outcome at the den changes what Caspian does next: pursue Ravka if she escaped, help the children if they accepted rescue, or reckon with a loss he could not undo. Do not reserve his entire arc for another boss fight in Arc Q. Ravka's fate should follow what happened at the table.
6. **The wider boundary (Act IV).** The Shadowfell resonance gains context through the Fanes, Amber Temple, and Strahd's plan with the Mists. Caspian can recognize or interpret a boundary disturbance and contribute a meaningful action, but the party's success must not depend on a special ability only he has. The final payoff is that he chooses to use his training to protect people from being treated as expendable by powers that cross worlds at will.
7. **Resolution.** After Strahd's defeat, Caspian decides what Ashcairn means to him now: return if there is something to return to, help those who survived, or carry its memory into a new purpose. The ending should resolve his chosen responsibility, not require an invented surviving NPC or presume the village can be rebuilt.

## What it gives the player

- **Want:** Find the attackers and get justice for Ashcairn.
- **Possible need:** Decide what his skill and anger are for when the immediate target is gone, dead, or not the whole cause. This is a proposed arc question, not a predetermined answer.
- **Choices:** Which leads to follow; whether to prioritize evidence, an immediate threat, or an opportunity to protect others; how to treat the taken children if they are encountered; and what to do with his connection to Ashcairn after Strahd falls.
- **Growth:** From pursuing one target as the answer to every loss, toward choosing his own standard for justice and protection. He can still choose vengeance; the arc should make its costs and alternatives legible, not force forgiveness.
- **Potential table scenes:** Tracking and investigating the pack, tactical encounters where his Magus/Order of the Blades training matters, conversations with witnesses, hard choices around the den, and a non-exclusive role in responding to the Mists. These are suggestions based on the character's established details, not assumptions about the player's preferred play style.

## How it differs from the other players

- **Agni:** His center is blood hunger, his son, and a multi-stage cure against Osric Valemont. Caspian's center is justice, responsibility, and the boundary between the Shadowfell and Barovia; the children are a possible den encounter, not a parallel child-cure campaign.
- **Ivan:** His center is family, clan, leadership, and the Huntress's inheritance. Caspian may share the den and the pack, but does not inherit Ivan's family revelations or become responsible for deciding the clan's future.
- **Sister Elga:** Her center is the Ven, the trapped dead, and whether her institution should preserve or release souls. Caspian's resonance is about living across planar boundaries and resisting powers that manipulate them, not shepherding the dead.
- **Grim:** Her center is finding Milo and the cost of a supernatural bargain. Caspian's resolution should not depend on recovering a missing child as the sole emotional payoff; the den children, if used, remain one decision among several.

The current plan's strongest overlap is the taken-children rescue beat. Keep it bounded to the den and make Caspian's broader arc about judgment and the planar boundary, not parenting, curing, or rebuilding someone else's family.

## Other directions considered

1. **The Unfinished Hunt:** Keep the story tightly focused on Ashcairn, Ravka, and the taken children. This offers the clearest personal stakes and a direct payoff, but it overlaps more with Agni's and Grim's child-centered rescue arcs and risks putting all of Caspian's story at Arc L.
2. **The Order's Missing Record:** Make Caspian's old academy text the main mystery, with Barovian sources gradually correcting what it says about Strahd and the Vistani. This gives him an investigation arc, but risks turning scenes into lore delivery and gives the Order little presence in the campaign.
3. **The Blade at the Threshold (recommended):** Use the Ashcairn hunt as the emotional engine, but spread clues and choices from Arc C onward; make Caspian's own Shadar-kai/Shadowfell connection the distinct long arc. This provides a way for him to matter before the den and at the climax without making him the campaign's secret solution.
