## Prey

You can designate one creature as your **Prey**. Your instincts guide you toward studying, pursuing, and defeating this creature.

When you make an unarmed strike using your Hybrid Form against your Prey, you use the next higher damage die than your Rite Die. For example, if your Rite Die is a d4, your unarmed strikes against your Prey deal damage using a d6 instead.

When you use your **Sinister Insight** ability against your Prey, you have advantage on the check.

You can have only one Prey at a time.

## Instinct

Your lycanthropic instincts wax and wane, determining how strongly you are connected to the Beast within you.

Your Instinct is measured in points. Your maximum Instinct is equal to **6 times your Blood Hunter level**.

Everyday at dawn, you lose Instinct equal to your Blood Hunter level.

Whenever you are reduced to 0 hit points but survive, you lose **5 Instinct**.

### The Hunt

When you designate a creature as your Prey, you begin a Hunt. During the Hunt, you gain **Hunt Points** whenever your Prey proves itself a worthy opponent.

You gain 1 Hunt Point for each round in which your Prey actively participates in the fight. You also gain 1 Hunt Point whenever your Prey:

- Causes an attack against it to miss.
- Hits with an attack.
- Succeeds on a saving throw.
- Causes you to fail a saving throw.
- Performs another feat that the DM deems worthy of the Hunt.

These points can be gained from the actions of any creature, not only you. The Hunt measures the strength and resistance of your Prey, not your own performance.

When you reduce your Prey to 0 hit points or otherwise incapacitate it, the Hunt ends. You gain **3 additional Hunt Points**, and your accumulated Hunt Points are retained until the end of combat.

If you designate a new Prey before defeating or incapacitating your current Prey, your current Hunt ends and you lose all Hunt Points accumulated during it.

### Converting Hunt Points

At the end of combat, you can convert your accumulated Hunt Points into Instinct. To do so, you must either feed upon your fallen Prey or perform a hunting ritual, both within 24 hours of the Hunt.

**Feeding.** You can gain the full amount of Instinct shown on the table by consuming the flesh of your slain Prey for **1 consecutive minute**.

**Hunting Ritual.** If you choose not to feed, you can perform a **10-minute ritual** to gain half the amount of Instinct shown on the table, rounded down.

| Hunt Points | Instinct |
| --- | --- |
| 0–4 | 0 |
| 5–8 | 5 |
| 9–12 | 10 |
| 13–16 | 15 |
| 17–20 | 20 |
| 21+ | 25 |

After converting your Hunt Points, your Hunt Point total resets to 0.

Your Instinct can't exceed your maximum.

## Instinct Stages

Your Instinct is divided into three stages: **Suppressed, Balanced, and Unleashed**. The effects of each stage are detailed below.

### Suppressed

Your connection to the Beast is weak, leaving your instincts unfocused.

- You can designate a creature as your Prey as a **Bonus Action**.
- You have **disadvantage on attack rolls against creatures other than your Prey**.

### Balanced

You and the Beast exist in harmony.

- You can designate a creature as your Prey as a **Bonus Action**.

### Unleashed

You embrace the Beast completely, allowing your predatory instincts to guide you without hesitation.

- You can designate a creature as your Prey **without using an action**.
- You gain **Pack Tactics** against your Prey.

## Pack Tactics

You have advantage on an attack roll against your Prey if at least one of your allies is within 5 feet of the creature and the ally isn't incapacitated.
