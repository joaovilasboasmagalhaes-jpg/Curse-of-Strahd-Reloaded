Your knowledge of Barovia comes primarily from your clan's traditions, the stories of your elders, and what you learned during your own time in the wilderness. Your clan has lived apart from most Barovian society for generations, so much of your knowledge is old, practical, or incomplete.

## The Land

Barovia is an ancient valley surrounded by mountains and forests. The wilderness is dangerous, particularly after sunset. You know of three major settlements: the Village of Barovia, Vallaki, and Krezk. The Village of Barovia lies in the eastern part of the valley, near the base of the mountains. Vallaki lies farther west, near the shores of Lake Zarovich. Krezk lies in the northwest, high in the mountains. You know roughly where they lie, but have little knowledge of their politics, customs, or recent affairs. The valley contains old ruins, forgotten roads, and places that animals avoid, some of which are believed to predate Strahd's rule. The Mists surround the valley and make leaving nearly impossible. Your clan has always treated them as something to be respected rather than understood.

## Strahd von Zarovich

**Strahd von Zarovich** is the lord of Barovia and rules from **Castle Ravenloft**. Your clan's traditional approach to Strahd has always been simple: **do not attract his attention**. Your elders taught that Strahd is ancient, powerful, and patient. The clan survived by remaining hidden rather than challenging him. You know that Strahd has a sway in the land and that the dead do not rest easily under his rule. Recently, however, something has changed. Strahd has become more active after a long period of relative quiet. More undead have also been seen in the woods. Your clan considers this unusual and dangerous, but you do not know why he has awakened or what caused the change. You know little about his personal history beyond the fact that he has ruled Barovia for generations.

## Omens and Superstitions

Barovians have many beliefs about signs and omens, and your clan has its own traditions. **Ravens are generally considered a good omen.** Their presence is often interpreted as a sign that something has gone right or that help is nearby. Wolves are treated with greater respect. To your people, a wolf watching from the forest is not automatically a threat; it may simply be observing. The Mists are another matter. Your elders taught you never to trust what you hear or see within them, and many old traditions warn against answering voices from the forest at night. Silver is known to be dangerous to werewolves and other creatures touched by lycanthropy.

## The Huntress

Your clan has worshipped the **Huntress** for generations. She is one of the ancient **Ladies**, four sister goddesses who once ruled the valley long before Strahd. She is the goddess of **hunting, war, summer, and young passion**, and her sacred symbol is the **wolf**. The Huntress was said to favor those who lived by the hunt. She taught that prey should be respected, strength should be tested, and a successful hunt should never be taken for granted.

According to your clan's traditions, the ancient **Wolfir** were the chosen hunters of the Huntress. They were werewolves who served her and joined her **Wild Hunt**. Unlike the werewolves feared throughout Barovia today, the Wolfir were considered good and honorable. Your elders taught that the wolf's nature was not originally a curse. **Lycanthropy was once a gift of the Huntress**, bestowed upon those worthy of joining her hunt.

The Huntress had three sisters. Together, the Huntress, the **Weaver**, and the **Seeker** were known as the **Ladies Three**. The Weaver represented spring, birth, family, and new life, and her symbol was the spider. The Huntress represented summer, hunting, war, and young passion, and her symbol was the wolf. The Seeker represented autumn, foresight, harvest, and the wisdom of age, and her symbol was the raven. The fourth sister, the **Dreamer**, represented winter, rest, hope, and the grave, and her symbol was the butterfly.

The old stories say that the Ladies once had sacred places throughout the valley where their power touched the land. Your clan remembers the **Forest Fane** as a place associated with the Huntress, though its exact location and purpose have been lost to time. Much of the ancient history has been forgotten. You know that the Wolfir eventually disappeared and that the Huntress is no longer openly worshipped throughout Barovia. Your elders never agreed on what became of her.

One teaching, however, has always remained: **The hunt is sacred. Respect your prey. Honor the strength it shows you.**

## The Wolfir

The Wolfir are the oldest werewolves your clan knows of. They were not infected by a curse. They were **chosen**. The Wolfir served the Huntress and were said to run beside her during the Wild Hunt. They retained their minds and used their wolf forms willingly. Your clan considers the distinction between **inherited lycanthropy** and **infected lycanthropy** extremely important. The Wolfir represent what lycanthropy was meant to be; the infected represent what it can become when the Beast is separated from its proper purpose.

## The First Folk

Your clan knows fragments of stories about an ancient people who lived in the valley long before Strahd. They were hunters and gatherers who lived close to the land and worshipped the Ladies. You know little more than that. The name **First Folk** survives in some old stories, as does the belief that many of the oldest sacred places in Barovia were created by them. The elders have always warned that much of what remains from that age should be treated with respect.
