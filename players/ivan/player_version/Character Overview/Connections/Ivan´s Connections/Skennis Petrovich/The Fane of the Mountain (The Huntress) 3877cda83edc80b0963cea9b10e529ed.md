# The Fane of the Mountain (The Huntress)

### The Divine Source

The First Folk of Barovia did not view nature as a gentle mother; they viewed it as a brutal, necessary cycle. The Huntress is said to be the Archfey who governs the Mountain Fane. She represents the season of Summer, the apex hunt, physical strength, raw passion, and the dark underside of nature—natural death. She is not evil, but to her, death is a sacred transaction meant to be embraced. In her true form, she is described as a tall, muscular, bark-skinned humanoid wearing a massive giant elk skull with wide, branching antlers, speaking only through the howling wind and the snapping of branches.

### The Wolfir Boon

The Petrovich clan's inherited lycanthropy is a fragment of the Huntress's divine spark. Generations ago, she blessed their bloodline with the "Wolfir Boon" so that the greatest hunters of the clan could turn into pure, apex predators and run alongside her during the Wild Hunt.

### The Desecration & The Fading Blood

Your clan teaches that a sacred circle atop Yester Hill was defiled by Strahd and his undead. Because she represents natural death, the undead are her ultimate abomination. This bloody, parasitic ritual is said to have shattered her physical presence, and many believe it strengthened Strahd’s influence over the valley itself, even affecting the weather.

Because Strahd’s influence is believed to interfere with the old source of her power, the line connecting the Huntress to her children has been frayed. This is why their kind is rare, why the pack is fading, and why the trauma of exile caused Ivan's shifting abilities to go dormant. Ivan's Blood Hunter rituals are not a curse—they are a grueling, makeshift bridge forced through his own blood to touch her dormant power once more. Deep in his subconscious, the beast screams for the blood of the vampire who corrupted its creator.

## 1. The Laws of the Pack & The Prey

### The Law of the Trophy (The Sacred Transaction)

Your clan believes that taking a life in the forest is not a right; it is a sacred exchange overseen by the Huntress. To take life without respect is to court her wrath.

- **The Custom:** Ivan should never carelessly leave a body to rot. Whenever he kills a natural beast for food or defense, he must perform a brief, silent ritual: placing a fresh leaf, a stone, or a handful of dirt over the creature’s eyes to shield its spirit as it passes back to the roots.
- **The Token:** He likely keeps a small bone needle, a tooth, or a claw from his first successful solo hunt woven into his hair or gear as a sign of his bond with her domain.

### The Law of the Clean Kill

Cruelty is an insult to the apex predator. The Huntress demands efficiency.

- **The Teaching:** Causing unnecessary suffering to prey is considered a sin. A true hunter strikes to end the fight instantly. Ivan favors swift, decisive strikes (perfectly matching the combat focus of a Blood Hunter). If an animal is dying slowly, he will immediately step in to grant it a quick end.

## 2. Primal Rituals of the Mountain

### The Smearing of the Brow (The Blood Rite)

Before an exceptionally dangerous hunt or battle, your clan invokes the raw passion and physical strength of the Summer Fane.

- **The Custom:** Ivan uses a small amount of blood—either his own or from a freshly harvested kill—to draw a single, vertical line down his forehead or across his eyes. This is a prayer asking the Huntress to clear his vision, heighten his scent, and grant his muscles the explosive strength of the wolf.

### The Full Moon Howl (The Shared Grief)

Because the full moon forces your kind into a wild, animalistic state where you lose your humanoid intellect, your mother Martha taught you a specific way to handle the aftermath.

- **The Teaching:** The morning after a full moon, the clan would gather at dawn in human form, completely silent, to watch the sun rise. They would then offer a single, collective howl in human voices. This custom honors the fact that while the beast takes over at night, the Huntress always allows them to return to their family by day. It is a ritual of grounding and re-centering the mind.

## 3. Philosophical Teachings: The Cycle of Death

### Natural Decay vs. The Unnatural Abomination

This is the core teaching that drives your Blood Hunter path, passed down fiercely by Old Skennis.

- **The Teaching:** The Huntress governs *natural* death. A body rotting in the dirt, feeding the insects and the soil, is beautiful and correct. **The Undead are a violent crime against her.** Vampires, zombies, and ghouls are parasites that freeze the cycle of life and death, trapping spirits in stagnant agony.
- **Roleplay Impact:** Ivan does not just find undead creatures "creepy"—he finds them profoundly offensive. He will view them as absolute rot that must be excised from the valley.

### The Whispering Wall & The Ancestors

Your clan does not believe in a distant heaven or hell.

- **The Teaching:** When a true child of the Huntress dies naturally, their spirit travels to the **Whispering Wall** (the permanent wall of fog surrounding Yester Hill). From there, their consciousness returns to the roots of the valley to watch over the forest. Your father taught you that your mother Martha is there. To free the Fane from Strahd is to ensure your mother's spirit—and the spirits of all your ancestors—are not corrupted by the vampire's shadow.

## 4. Taboos (What Ivan Must *Never* Do)

- **Never Fight with Silver:** Your clan completely bans the use of silver weapons among your own kind. Even though you are inherited lycanthropes and have immense control, silver is seen as a cowardly, toxic metal that "burns the divine boon."
- **Never Blind a Predator:** To gouge the eyes of a wolf, a bear, or an owl out of malice is an unforgivable taboo. The eyes are the Huntress's gift to her hunters.
- **Never Deny the Night:** You were taught never to curse your lycanthropy. Even when it brings pain, exile, or hardship, it is a piece of a goddess inside you. To hate the beast is to hate the Huntress.
