Ivan Petrovich, that is my name. I am the son of Old Skennis, an inherited Werewolf that was, for several decades, responsible for a Werewolf Pack that is housed in the Svalich Woods south of the Village of Barovia. From them, I received a harsh education. The power of being an inherited Werewolf came from a deal from the Fane of the Mountain, the Huntress, a powerful being that enjoyed mortals so much they gave them the boon of Inherited Werewolf Lycanthropy.

Inherited lycanthropes can change forms at will and retain their full personality and mind when in their altered states. The only time when this is not the case is on the night of a full moon, in which all inherited lycanthropes are forced to change into their were-form and lose control for an evening, following their animalistic instincts. However, even then they aren't nearly as violent as infected lycanthropes and can recognize friends and family. We are told to avoid them. We do not speak to them. We do not seek them out.

Inherited lycanthropes undergo their first change at a very young age, usually around four or five years old. With enough practice and focus, I gained full control of my change in a few short years. However, this transformed state is age-appropriate. For instance, a young werewolf will change into a puppy version of a wolf while still an adolescent. Because this change is a blessing, inherited lycanthropes experience minimal pain during shifts.

My early life has been nothing but sweat and blood, I was taught to master my body and my mind to its fullest potential. The soldiers responsible for my education were ruthless and hours of training felt like hell, but compared to my father´s lectures that was playtime. Although in my youth I resented my father for such harsh treatment, over the years I learned to accept and embrace the gift in my bloodline and be grateful for everything I´ve been through. I deny it, but I truly thank my father for all those years of patience and dedication to me and my younger brothers, **Emil** and **Kiril**. As the oldest, I always felt like I had a more intense relationship with my father - it was obvious he was preparing me for the future as a possible leader of the pack. As I grew up, I acknowledged this responsibility even though I never truly felt like a worthy leader, nor did I want the title. I am a focused worker, no task is too big or too small in my hands, but leading others… I know my limits, or at least I think I do.

I was always sincere with my father in all matters and did everything I was told no questions asked until he referred the opportunity to retire and for me to take his place. I didn´t refuse, but I didn´t accept either… I abstained from my answer for the time being but was emotionally ready to take his place in respect for the decades of service my father honorably provided.

In the impending weeks, the rumors clung to the air like an ominous mist. Whispers of my impending leadership stirred restlessness within the pack, and little did I know, they also kindled a fire of jealousy in my youngest brother, Kiril. For years, I'd borne the weight of my father's teachings, enduring grueling training and harsh lessons that molded me into the lycan I am today. But as fate would have it, the mantle of leadership loomed on the horizon, a responsibility I never sought and never truly believed I deserved.

Kiril, fueled by envy and an insatiable hunger for power, chose a darker path. He sought an alliance with Strahd, the malevolent force that plagued our cursed land. The pact he made, however, was one that would tear our family apart.

One haunting night, as the moon cast its eerie glow upon the land, Kiril confronted me. His eyes, once familiar, now glowed with an unnatural malevolence. The revelation of his alliance with Strahd hung in the air like a poisonous fog, and he delivered his ultimatum with a cold determination.

He threatened to unleash Strahd's wrath upon our pack unless I willingly surrendered my claim to leadership. The betrayal cut deep, a wound that resonated with the loyalty I thought ran through our pack's veins.

The choice was stark—abandon my birthright and the pack I'd grown to love or defy my own flesh and blood to protect them. Fearing the life of my loved ones, I chose to exile pretending I was ashamed of not being ready to take over the pack.

Kiril demanded I leave that same night and threatened to fulfill his deadly promise if I ever came back or tried to inform the pack about his deal with Stradh. In a desperate plea, I requested a single favor — to let me write a letter to the pack explaining a lie we concocted, sparing them the truth of our bitter conflict. I thanked Kiril for everything he had done, for the years of brotherhood and shared memories, even though they were now tainted by betrayal.

As I left, a knot of shame twisted in my gut. I realized I had been blind to a side of Kiril that had festered in the shadows. The jealousy, the thirst for power — how had I not seen it before? I was ashamed of my ignorance, of failing to recognize the signs of a sibling consumed by ambition.

As I left the pack's territory in the forest south of Barovia, I couldn't shake the heavy burden of sorrow. The betrayal of my own blood had left scars that would not easily heal. The moonlit night witnessed not only the departure of a potential leader but the fracture of a family, and the echoes of that fateful night lingered in the haunted woods. The journey ahead was uncertain, but I vowed to protect my pack from afar, carrying the weight of my brother's choices like a somber shroud. Although anger and sorrow were understandable emotions, a feeling of uneasiness enveloped my mind. Something felt off about Kiril, he clearly was not himself. No amount of jealousy could have torn our bond. Maybe I was being naive, but one thing was certain - Kiril was influenced by Stradh.

Kiril, my kin, you might not realize it but you need me one last time… And Stradh, I hope you´re ready because I will not rest until I end this burden.

After days of wandering through the Svalich Woods, I no longer know where I am. The mist thickens between the trees, and even familiar instincts fail me. I hunt when I can, sleep when exhaustion forces me, and keep moving without direction. Every path feels the same. Every silence feels wrong.

One night, I hear howls in the distance. At first, I hesitate. They sound like my kind. For a moment, I allow myself to believe I might have found them, or that they might have found me. But the hope does not last.

The howls come closer.

Wolves emerge from the fog, but they are not kin. They move with intent, too coordinated, too persistent. Something drives them forward that is not natural. I run.

They do not stop following me.

Eventually, I break through the treeline onto an old road cutting through the forest. Ahead, a caravan moves through the mist—bright colors, lanterns, figures I do not recognize. I do not know what they are, only that they are not part of the woods.
