# Concept: The Fire and the Command

> [!info] Concept file
> Status: `exploring`. This is a dry-run proposal, not campaign canon. No campaign files or DM plans have been changed.

## Idea

Use the Tser Pool visit to let Elga compare Abbot Ferd's old dream with the Vistani's present-day fire and stories. The scene gives her a personal moment and a question, not an immediate explanation of the Priory's arrival or a new hag quest. Later, the soul bag can echo the Urn's purpose while remaining Morgantha's and Grim's story.

No `player_profile.md` exists. The DM states the player likes roleplay and appreciates lore drops and backstory involvement, but lore cannot be dropped constantly. This concept therefore favors a few well-placed, in-character moments over a steady stream of revelations.

## Ferd's Reasoning: Options

### A. A Perceived Danger, a Mistaken Target (recommended)

Ferd experienced the dream as a warning that the Priory or the souls it served were in danger. He interpreted its fire, music, and dancing people as signs of a hostile soul-working, then ordered the newly ordained Elga to stop its source. Whether there was any real rite or supernatural threat is unresolved; do not turn his interpretation into objective fact. His order may have authorized lethal force against the people he believed responsible, but it was not an order to kill every Vistana at the camp.

This gives Ferd a reason beyond protecting Elga while leaving him accountable for treating an ambiguous dream as evidence and giving a novice an absolute-sounding command. The camp scene can show that the Vistani's present-day stories do not confirm his interpretation; it cannot prove what Ferd saw centuries ago.

### B. Destroy the Rite, Not the People

Ferd meant Elga to destroy a suspected rite or its instrument, and his phrase about dealing with heresy "one way" referred to that target. Elga reasonably understood him as ordering violence against the people involved. This preserves her memory of the assignment but gives Ferd less culpability. It risks softening the explicit command in the fixed backstory, so use only if the DM wants that interpretation and confirms it still fits what Elga remembers.

### C. A Vision Distorted by Fear or the Convergence

Ferd received a real vision, but the Convergence or another force distorted its imagery and urgency. He believed he had a sound reason to act, while the warning was incomplete or misleading. This makes the event more tragic and leaves room for a later source to be involved, but risks shifting responsibility away from Ferd or adding another hidden actor to an already dense mystery.

**DM preference: Option C, refined.** Ferd received two inputs at the same moment: the image of the dancing Vistani by a fire, and a separate feeling of evil. He fused them into one meaning, though they were unrelated. The source of the evil is left open: Strahd, Barovia itself, the Dark Powers, the Mists, or Mother Night. The Vistani were innocent bystanders in his vision; the evil was elsewhere. This makes the tragedy a misreading (a conflation), not a lie or a hidden Vistani crime, and Ferd remains accountable for acting absolutely on an unverified vision.

Consequences for play: the Vistani can honestly be innocent of what Ferd feared, and nothing at the camp needs to be a hidden threat. The true source of the evil feeling stays undefined until the DM chooses (Open Question 1). Eva may hold part of the answer and withhold it.

Risk to manage: do not let the unseen "other force" overshadow Ferd's own decision to treat a feeling as proof and give a novice a deadly order.

## Arc Beats

1. **Tser Pool: recognition and a small, permitted set of facts.** Eva *can* say, if the moment is right and Elga asks: that Elga is late; that she met Ferd briefly; and the rough location of the Priory in the valley. She is not required to say any of it. She will probably hint that she knows more, especially when she recognizes Elga as a sister. She withholds the rest (the dream's meaning, the reading, the Convergence) in character as a mysterious person; what exactly she holds back is undecided (Open Question 2). This revises the DM plan's knowledge boundary: Elga may learn the Priory is nearby in the valley, but not what happened to it.
2. **The fire: Elga chooses whether to share.** During the existing Game of Stories, Elga may tell the Abbot's dream, ask what a Vistani story or fire means to its teller, or keep the memory to herself. Do not ask the Vistani to explain a curse; Arc C says they do not discuss Vistani curses. Their present-day stories may differ from Elga's dream, but cannot prove what happened centuries ago or what Ferd believed.
3. **A private choice with Eva.** If Elga asks whether Ferd's dream was true, Eva declines to disclose his reading. Keep the existing evening-prayer joke as part of the scene. Any observation must be her own limited comment, not confirmation of the dream's meaning, not a hag secret, and not the Priory's fate or the rune's origin.
4. **Later echo at Old Bonegrinder (Arc H).** If Elga is present when Morgantha's soul bag is discovered or discussed, she can compare its use with the Urn: the hag keeps a corrupted soul as a trophy or resource, while the Ven's rite is meant to listen to and honor the dead. This is a thematic contrast, not evidence that the hags caused the Convergence or that the Vistani camp served them.
5. **Shared thread, separate spotlights.** Arc H already has Morgantha's soul-leech plan for Rahadin: if the leech attaches, his soul will be caught in her bag when he dies. Elga's personal role is her later choice to Unbind Rahadin's choir; Grim's personal role remains the confrontation with Morgantha and the meaning of the coven's bag. Elga does not identify Morgantha as Grim's culprit, decide what happens to Grim's old crew, or become necessary to Grim's rescue of Milo.

## What It Gives the Player

- **Want:** Understand what happened to the Priory and why Ferd sent her away.
- **Need, as a possible arc:** Learn to test an authority's interpretation against evidence and her own judgment, rather than treating obedience as proof of faith. This is a proposed character direction, not a claim about the player's preference.
- **Choices:** Share or withhold the dream; challenge the Vistani or listen; ask Eva for help or respect her limits; later, decide what the Urn's contrast with the soul bag means to Elga.
- **Growth and payoff:** The early scene gives Elga a question to carry. The later bag encounter can make her doctrine concrete without taking the hag plot from Grim. The campaign's existing Priory and Rahadin threads remain her main personal arc.
- **Fun scene:** A campfire story in which Elga can take the floor, decide what part of her past to reveal, and hear a response without being forced into a fight or confession.

## How It Differs from the Other Players

- **Grim:** Her core antagonist and emotional stakes are Morgantha's coven, Milo, and the soul bag. Elga's core remains the Ven, Ferd, her doctrine, and Rahadin. The bag is a brief thematic mirror; no Elga reveal determines Grim's culprit, reunion, or choice.
- **Agni:** His core is Osric Valemont, Milosh, and a cure pursued through alchemy. Elga may help with alchemy, but this camp scene is about her faith and interpretation of an order, not a child's cure.
- **Caspian:** His arc is the werewolf raid on Ashcairn and the children taken from home. Elga's scene is not another child-rescue or revenge lead.
- **Ivan:** His arc concerns divided family, return to the pack, and the Huntress. Elga's home/order is also a community, but her central question is what the dead deserve and how she judges doctrine.
