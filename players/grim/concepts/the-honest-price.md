## Player baseline (from the DM and Zuka's conversation)

Source: a raw conversation in Portuguese between the DM and Zuka, Grim's player, supplied by the DM on 2026-10-17. This is the closest thing to a profile for Grim. The DM should copy what applies into `player_profile.md`; I have not created it.

- **The baseline:** an evil creature stole her child. The other parent died in the incident. The creature is native to Barovia and went back there.
- **The pouch:** it holds the child's beating heart so she knows the child is alive. The idea came from the trinket table.
- **The deal:** there is a deal between Grim and this entity to get the child back, but what she really wants is to kill it. She refers to the creature with a feminine form.
- **Priority:** asked whether she wants to kill it more than she wants the child, the player said no. The child comes first.
- **Scale:** one or two more characters may be involved, but she does not want to complicate it too much.
- **The creature:** the player wants an important creature, not a random one, with an easy tie-in to the campaign. She floated a moderately relevant Baron, a hag leader of a coven, or a werewolf, and said the specifics can be worked out together later.

What this tells us: she wants the child back **and** a named culprit she can kill, tied into the campaign.

**How the DM reads this baseline (2026-10-17):**
- **"Keep it simple"** is probably an attempt to save the DM effort, not a real player preference. It is not treated as a constraint.
- **"One creature that stole, killed, and dealt"** comes from not knowing Barovia's lore. It is a working hypothesis to keep in mind, not a rule. The plan is not discarded, but the reveal must satisfy a player who expected one creature.
- **"Native to Barovia and returned"** reflects not knowing the laws of Barovia. It does not need to be enforced.

## Idea

Grim wants Milo back, and her player also wants her to find and kill the creature that took him and killed Will. The hags' revenge for the old swamp fight was to take her son, kill her husband, and leave her alive and shattered. The Mists saw her devastation and offered her a deal, as the Dark Powers offered Tatyana a choice: accept a path, or stay broken. She accepted. The hags do not know about the deal. The deal is honest: nothing the Mists said is false, and it never promised a reunion. The arc gives her a named, killable culprit with ties to the campaign, who is not the author of the bargain, then makes her weigh the kill against Milo, which her player has said ranks second.

Lore decisions live in [dm_plans.md](../dm_plans.md), Section 2. The Mists as a separate, non-violent dealmaker now matches this concept. See Open decisions for what still needs changing there.

## Arc beats

1. **Setup (Arc C and Act II).** The culprit is hinted through things Grim can sense and test (the pouch, the shadow, the mark) before it is named. Her investigation stays partial.
2. **Escalation (Act II into Act III).** The culprit's reach and the deal's terms become clearer, mirrored by child-taking and bargaining around her.
3. **Choice.** She gets within reach of the kill, but the culprit holds what Grim needs (where Milo went), so taking the kill costs her information or time. She has said Milo ranks first, so the choice should be real, not a trap.
4. **Payoff.** A confrontation where she can rescue Milo and, if she plays it well, kill the creature. Which she gets, and at what cost, depends on her choices.

Pacing depends on who the culprit is (see below). Stage-by-stage scenes in dm_plans.md Sections 3 and 4 will need revising once that is settled.

## What it gives the player

- **What she wants (stated by the player).** Milo back, and the creature dead, in that order of priority.
- **What the arc proposes she needs (the DM's call).** To keep the caution that protects people while not letting desperation turn others, or Milo, into the price.
- **Key choices.** Which leads to follow, what to share with the party, how to answer the culprit's deal, and whether to take the kill when it risks Milo.
- **Growth (proposed).** She starts as a pragmatic, desperate parent who argued for the Death House ritual and was prepared to risk Walter. She ends choosing deliberate courage. Check with the player that this is the growth she wants.
- **Scenes built from established material.** Negotiation (her silver tongue), investigating the pouch and the mark, using the Living Shadow to reach where she cannot (as with the chest in Session 2), and a confrontation she has been working toward.
- **Table reality.** Every clue is something she can find through play: the pouch, the shadow, Madam Eva, Fiona, the culprit's own words.

## Culprit options, ranked by what this player would enjoy most

The backstory says the hags killed some of his old crew and that he never learned what happened to the bodies, so this is a payoff for something he wrote himself. What makes it cool:

Risk: the confrontation is in Act II. The fix is that Milo is not at Old Bonegrinder, so killing her does not end the search, and her daughters or Lysaga can carry the story afterward.

**2. She is detached and cannot feel anger, a cold foil for a grieving player. Her mist and Mistfiend form match the mist that came over Grim's town, and the confrontation is a real boss fight in Arc P (Act III), so the arc runs longer. The costs: nothing in his backstory points to her, she would have to have left the valley (a mist talisman or an excursion during Strahd's sleep), and Strahd would need to stay ignorant.

**3. The werewolf (Zuleika or the pack).** Matches his joke, but Arc L already belongs to Caspian and Ivan. Not recommended.

**4. A Baron.** None obviously fits. Drop unless he has a specific one in mind.

### Making the reveal satisfying

Zuka expects one creature to be both killer and dealer. The decided structure lets him be right about the visible figure and learn the larger truth later:
- **The deal is the Mists'.** It mirrors Tatyana's. The Dark Powers do not force anyone; they offer a choice at the worst moment and the person accepts. Tatyana faced Strahd on one side and death on the other. Grim had just lost Will and Milo. The hags' revenge was to leave her alive and shattered, and the Mists saw an opening.
- **The hags don't know.** They believe their revenge worked. Morgantha's surprise at seeing Grim in Barovia is real, and when Grim demands to know about "the deal," her confusion is honest. She cannot confess what she does not know.
- **The shadow is the clue.** The Living Shadow is a fragment of the shadow Morgantha's hulking form left behind, worn by the Mists to speak the terms. It reacts to her, and her own shadow may look wrong near it. The figure's flat, ritual speech doesn't match her warm voice. Grim can notice these before she knows what they mean.
- **The kill still happens.** Morgantha's coven took Milo and killed Will. Killing her does not end the bargain and is not required by it, so the kill is Grim's to take or trade for information. Arc H's parley is the natural moment.
- **The surprise.** Grim learns the thing she wanted to kill never wrote the deal. Killing the hag answers the murder but not the bargain. That is a surprise on top of the kill, not a correction.
- **Optional echo.** The Tatyana parallel can surface through Ireena or Madam Eva without being explained, so the player feels the rhyme before the DM names it.

## Standing constraint: Sister Elga

Morgantha's interest in Rahadin's soul ties Grim's arc to Sister Elga's. Check [Elga's plan](../../sister_elga/dm_plans.md) before changing anything here. The facts that bind us:
- Rahadin cannot die while his chorus of dusk elf souls stays bound. Elga's Unbinding frees them (Arc T); only then does he become mortal ([Rahadin notes](../../sister_elga/rahadin.md)).
- Souls cannot leave Barovia while the Mists hold. Passing and Unbinding give local farewells and sever local holds. Shepherding to Kelemvor waits until the Mists fall.
- Elga's plan already flags a shared Arc P Heart-tower moment for Grim (section 9). Check before leaning on it.
- Elga's Urn is a soul vessel. Morgantha's soul bag is a rival one.

What the link adds:
- **Morgantha's plan depends on Elga.** Until the choir is unbound Rahadin can't die, so her soul larva waits on Elga's success. After Unbinding, the freed souls drift loose in Barovia, which is exactly what a soul bag collects.
- **Grim's kill gains a purpose beyond revenge.** Stopping Morgantha protects the souls Elga frees.
- **The old crew's souls (Q9) fit.** If they are in Morgantha's soul bag, killing her spills them. Elga can offer a Passing and a local farewell, not an escape, which gives Grim closure without breaking Elga's ruling.
- **Morgantha may covet the Urn.** A soul vessel is a hag's natural prize, so she will notice it.
- **A pouch beside an urn.** Both hold something living or spiritual, an echo for Arc P. The Dark Powers cannot directly act upon souls, so the pouch should not be a soul trap (Q3).

## Revisited options (after researching the coven and Lysaga)

**Culprit.** Morgantha's coven is now the clear choice. Ludmilla's advantage (an Act III boss fight) is covered better by the Elga convergence, and the werewolf and Baron stay dropped.

**Relationship (decided).** The coven reveres Lysaga as the legendary Witch Mother and means Milo as tribute for her (already delivered or still pending; see below), seeking protection from Strahd's notice and her rites. Lysaga accepts with condescension. Morgantha privately resents kneeling to a human witch, which makes her a plausible informant. Milo lives because he is worth more as tribute than as a meal. This is separate from the Mists' deal, which the hags do not know about.

**When does Grim get her kill? (the players decide; prepare both; nudge toward F2)**

Nudges toward F2, without forcing it: Morgantha's parley can include a hint that Milo lives and that only she can open the door to him. Hags keep deals to the letter, so she can truthfully say that if she dies no one will wake him. Killing the coven early then costs Grim something real (see Milo below), while the parley is the only way to keep the door open. If the table kills the coven anyway, F1 plays out as described.
- **F1. In Arc H.** The standard fight uses Lady Wachter's binding circle, which blocks Morgantha's etherealness escape. After the hags are bloodied, Morgantha calls a parley: three secrets for the coven's lives. If the party refuses, the coven fights to the death. Their hit points are pooled, so all three die together. The payoff is early, and Morgantha's Rahadin plan is moot.
- **F2. At the Mountain Fane in Arc T (preferred).** The Mountain Fane's megalith circle sits at the base of Old Bonegrinder's hill (Morgantha's second secret). Rahadin's Vengeance is fought there, so the coven is at home when Elga unbinds the choir. Morgantha survives Arc H because the table accepts the parley or takes the bargain route (heartstone loan, no fight). She then comes to harvest the freed souls and Rahadin's soul, and Grim kills her there. The kill is late and it protects Elga's work.

Correction to an earlier note: Morgantha does not escape into the Ethereal Plane in Arc H when the circle is up. F2 depends on the parley being accepted or the bargain route, not an escape.

Risk for F2: if Grim attacks Morgantha in Arc C, the coven can overwhelm a level-3 party. Keep the Living Shadow's reaction ambiguous so Grim lacks proof until Arc H.

**Milo as a character, not a prize (DM requirement).** The test is Finding Nemo, not Taken: Milo is a person with his own want, his own actions, and scenes of his own, and he reappears regularly so the audience feels his side. Out of reach does not mean offstage. Hide him until the end and he becomes a trophy; reach him too early and the arc dies. Alternate hope and dread so the stress doesn't flatten.

Milo's want and need (proposed; confirm with Zuka): he wants to go home and keep the younger captives safe, and he needs to stop waiting to be rescued and act. He is his father's son, a smith's child who thinks with his hands. One clever deed of his own should help save him at the climax.

### Morgantha's reaction (decided 2026-10-18)

**Why she stays composed:**
- She does not know what Grim knows or how she reached Barovia. A mortal following her here suggests a backer she cannot see (Strahd, the Mists, something else), so she tests before she commits.
- She is alone, the party is large, and Arc C already has her avoid fights (Morale: parley, then Etherealness).
- A brawl on the road risks Milo, her tribute for Lysaga, and draws Strahd's notice.
- She believes her revenge worked. Grim shattered and desperate is the point, and she enjoys watching it.
- Her cover is her best way to learn what Grim knows.

**At the scene:**
- The only tell is a half-beat pause on first sight, and her eyes on the pouch. No change in warmth.
- She fishes: *"Where do you come from, dear? You've the look of someone who's lost something."* She pays attention to how Grim answers, and savors the desperation.
- If Grim asks about Milo or children, she deflects pleasantly and gives nothing. She never confirms or denies. The lead is that she keeps showing up, not that she says anything.
- Grim's Living Shadow stirs near her. Only Grim feels it; Morgantha does not see it as a clue.

**Actions she takes to learn more (all quiet, none a fight):**
1. **The free pastry.** She presses a dream pastry on Grim in particular. Eaten, the trance gives her a look into Grim's mind and what she knows.
2. **Watching the party.** She slips into the Ethereal Plane to follow the party's camp or the road for a night, and may send Bella or Offalia to listen in Vallaki. She does not do anything that endangers her cover.
3. **Nightmare haunting.** A night-hag dream visit to Grim, with Milo's voice or face as bait to see what she reacts to. This is a canon hag ability and it feeds the Dreaming Boy beats (decoy Milo).
4. **Staying close.** She keeps her stall outside Vallaki's gates, where Grim can find her again and she can watch Grim.

What changes her mind: Grim proves she knows what Morgantha is (then she parleys and escapes as in Arc C's Morgantha Detected), or an attack (Etherealness escape).
