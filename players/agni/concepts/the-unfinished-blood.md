# Concept: The Unfinished Blood

> [!info] Concept file
> Status: `integrated`. The decisions below were applied to the campaign and Agni's plan files on 2026-10-18. This file still uses "Cassian" as a working name for Osric Valemont.

## Idea

Agni begins with a concrete goal: save Milosh. The arc keeps that goal present across the campaign with no contact between Agni and Milosh until the Mists part. Agni investigates what happened, confronts Cassian over what he did to the family, and faces choices shaped by his vampiric thirst. The village's other victims can create competing demands on his time and help, without displacing Milosh as the emotional anchor. No letters, couriers, or medicine deliveries cross the Mists; the separation is part of the pressure.

The deeper question is not whether Agni loves his son enough to sacrifice everything. That is already clear. It is what Agni believes "saving" Milosh means, and whether he can choose what to do with his hunger and his future while the cure remains out of reach.

## What Is Established

- **Backstory:** Agni's home is Widow's Peak. Mavis is dead; Milosh survived the blood-hunger episode and remains with the neighbors. Agni's stated aim is to find Vasili and cure Milosh. He describes himself as beyond redemption and says he only lives to save his son.
- **Moral boundary:** Agni has not fed on a living humanoid. His backstory does say he drank blood from Mavis after her death, so the current DM-plan rule should consistently define the threshold as feeding on living humanoid blood, not simply ever tasting human blood.
- **Session 7:** Agni and the party have already encountered Doru. Agni participated in Doru's blood test; Doru found Agni's blood different and less appealing. Grim gave blood for the test, and Doru resisted it. Do not replay this as a future first encounter.
- **Session 7:** Strahd has just introduced himself at the crossroads. He sniffed the air while looking at Agni and Ivan. The log does not say he recognized Cassian's work or knew about Milosh.
- **Documents:** Agni has mutable DM plans and campaign integration, but no `player_profile.md`, `design_notes.md`, or existing concept file.

## Options

### A. The Unfinished Blood (Provisional Recommendation)

**Shape:** A slow-burn investigation and family arc, with Agni's thirst creating active decisions along the way. The cure develops through play and is delivered in person after the Mists part.

1. **Now, Arc C:** Strahd's attention makes Agni's condition feel visible, but he does not identify Cassian or reveal knowledge he has no source for. Agni chooses whether to mention Vasili, his hunger, or Milosh during the conversation. The Doru test has already happened and remains an early mirror, not a future beat.
2. **Act II:** Agni follows evidence of the Widow's Peak experiment and works with Elga on what the research can actually accomplish. The village's other victims may need help; Agni chooses how much to take on while pursuing Milosh. His thirst, restraint, and Blood Sense create scenes where his methods and priorities matter. Agni has no news of Milosh, which keeps the worry and the hunger-driven choices on him.
3. **Act III:** Cassian's account of the failed cohorts and premature activation raises the personal stakes. Agni decides when to resist or use the blood connection, what risks to accept, and what to do with the research after Cassian is defeated.
4. **Act IV and resolution:** The deeper cure becomes possible. After Strahd falls and the Mists part, Agni can return to Milosh and offer it; Milosh's consent and response remain part of the payoff.

**What it gives Agni:** Milosh remains the heart of the story, while the other victims give Agni consequential choices beyond a single cure quest. His hunger, restraint, Blood Sense, and Cassian's Pull matter in play. The proposed growth is from self-condemnation and sole-responsibility toward deciding how to live with his condition and accepting help without surrendering his agency.

**Distinctive angle:** Unlike Grim's search for a taken child through a hag bargain or Caspian's justice/rescue conflict over raided children, Agni's arc is about a father pursuing treatment for a condition he and his son share, while deciding how to act when his own hunger and other people's needs compete. Elga's alchemy is a party bond, while her main story remains the Ven, souls, and Rahadin.

**Risks:** It can become a fetch-quest cure recipe or diffuse into a generic village-rescue plot. Keep Milosh central, let Agni decide which other victims to help, and make thirst-driven choices change events rather than merely call for repeated saving throws. Do not promise a cure or treatment outcome before the relevant choices and research pay it off.

### B. Widow's Peak Remembers

**Shape:** Broaden the story from Agni and Milosh to the small community Cassian experimented on. Cassian's notes and what Agni learns in Barovia reveal different outcomes among the surviving residents. Agni can choose to prioritize Milosh, help other victims, expose Cassian's work, or rebuild trust with neighbors who may blame him for leaving.

**What it gives Agni:** A concrete way to cause change in Barovia's wider story and a communal legacy beyond curing his son.

**Distinctive angle:** This is a responsibility-and-reconstruction arc, not primarily a personal nemesis hunt. Other PCs' family stories remain their own.

**Risks:** It could diffuse the emotional focus from Milosh or become another rescue-the-victims plot beside Grim and Caspian. Keep the survivor group small and make support optional, not a prerequisite to Milosh's cure.

### C. The Hunger Is His, the Choice Is Agni's

**Shape:** Center the arc on Agni's threshold and the soft blood connection. Cassian pressures him through hunger and memory; Agni chooses whether to resist or use the connection. The cure becomes a decision about whether Agni wants to be fully mortal, retain controlled Dhampir abilities, or leave that answer open.

**What it gives Agni:** Regular, varied use of his defining mechanics and a direct contest with the person who claims ownership of his body. This can enrich Option A without replacing Milosh as its emotional center.

**Distinctive angle:** The antagonist's conflict is about bodily autonomy and self-definition, not Ivan's inherited curse or Elga's doctrine.

**Risks:** It can sideline Milosh, become repetitive if Cassian's Pull fires too often, or dictate that the player's "growth" means accepting the curse. Treat it as a gameplay thread within Option A unless the player prefers a mechanics-forward personal arc.

## Review of Current DM Plan

The strongest existing pieces are the contrast with Doru, the staged cure, the soft blood connection, and Elga's alchemical collaboration. The main weakness is pacing: the plan gives Agni a strong Doru scene, then puts the next substantial personal beats in Vallaki and Ravenloft, with most metaphysical answers held for the Amber Temple and finale. Agni's hunger and decisions about the village's other victims can bridge that gap without relying on frequent contact with Milosh outside Barovia.

The plan also needs continuity decisions before being treated as settled:

- **Vasili is already Strahd's alias (DM decision: rename Strahd's alias).** Strahd's "Vasili von Holtz" appears in the History, Arc K, the timeline and the original book (N4q letter, Henrik's shop, Abbey history). It is a disguise with no plot weight beyond the handwriting reveal, so it can be renamed. Agni's fixed backstory keeps "Vasili" as the name his enemy used.
- **The antagonist's name is changing (DM decision).** "Cassian" is too close to the player character Caspian. Only mutable files use it (Agni's plans and this concept, Caspian's `dm_plans.md` line 23 and concept, Elga's concept); no log or fixed document does. The new name and surname are Osric Valemont (see "Names" below); this file keeps "Cassian" as a working name.
- **Cassian's crossing (DM decision: mist talisman).** See "The Mist Talisman" below.
- **Outside and Barovian time (DM decision).** The Mists displace arrival time and there is no fixed ratio, matching the proposed default in Grim's concept. For Agni the awakening was yesterday; Cassian entered Barovia three months ago on the Barovian clock. Do not convert Barovian months into outside days or build a countdown on it. Milosh's care runs on the neighbors' clock, and Cassian's years outside are his own elapsed time, not tied to the century of slumber.
- **The failed cohorts need not be fully detailed yet.** Cassian's future research notes can reveal their number, locations, and outcomes as the party investigates. Until then, the concept only needs them as evidence that Widow's Peak was the latest trial.
- **Correspondence is removed (DM decision).** No letters, couriers, *Sending*, or medicine deliveries between Agni and Milosh. The campaign sets no courier route or inside/outside time rate, so this avoids undefined timing.
- **Agni's cure threshold should use consistent wording.** The planned distinction is that Milosh drank from a living humanoid and crossed the threshold; Agni drank from Mavis after her death and has not fed on living humanoid blood. Keep that exact rule in future descriptions.

## The Mist Talisman (Proposed)

Status: provisional. The rules below are what the sources state (*Van Richten's Guide to Ravenloft* "Mist Talismans", "The Mists", the Barovia Darklord entry, "Travelers in the Mists"; [Arc T](../../Act%20IV%20-%20Secrets%20of%20the%20Ancient/Arc%20T%20-%20The%20Three%20Fanes.md#L1609); [History of Barovia](../../Chapter%202%20-%20The%20Land%20of%20Barovia/History%20of%20Barovia.md#L419)).

**What the sources establish**
- **What it is.** A nonmagical object, "akin to a dowsing rod or a lodestone", that resonates with the nature of the domain it comes from. The holder focuses on that origin and reaches it after 2d6 hours in the Mists; anyone who willingly follows arrives too. One talisman points to one destination.
- **Form.** Ominous and unique: a burned family holy book, a stuffed toy, a ring, any Horror Trinket. Khazan's platinum ring is the campaign's example.
- **Making.** No source gives a method. They are found, taken, bought or passed on, never crafted. The nearest thing to creation is the God-Brain's "Mist Vibrations", a Darklord power, and the Mist Traveler Dark Gift, which acts as a talisman for any named domain.
- **Who has them.** Few people know how to use one. Sources: Vistani families and Madam Eva (she gave Van Richten one, and a fortuneteller sold him Khazan's ring); the Keepers of the Feather (whose Mark of the Raven is itself a Barovia talisman); fey merchants of the Carnival's Litwick Market, who sell them for odd prices such as dreams; and travelers with the Mist Wanderer background, who recognize one on touch.
- **Barovia's own.** A Barovian wine bottle, the von Zarovich crest, the Mark of the Raven. These lead into Barovia.
- **Borders.** With open borders, leaving without a talisman takes 1d6 hours and then a roll on the Wandering the Mists table (only a 20% chance of a chosen destination). A talisman is no help to someone held inside closed borders, and if the destination's borders are closed the holder rolls on that table instead. The campaign says talismans work only while Strahd is dormant, and that he has closed the borders since waking.
- **Strahd's awareness.** He knows when any creature enters Barovia or dies violently there. Nothing says he knows when one leaves.

**Fit for Cassian (proposed)**

- **Origin (DM decision: his own).** Cassian was born in Widow's Peak itself, and the "born here a long time ago" claim in Agni's backstory is the true part of his cover story. The house beside his tavern was his family's. He was a physician there before the Mists carried him into Barovia, where Strahd, who knows when anyone enters, took him in and turned him. The talisman is a keepsake he carried across: a family heirloom or a piece of his physician's kit. No third party supplies it, and it points to his home, so Widow's Peak is not a coincidence. Whether any villager descends from him is undecided, and I would leave it out.
- **Knowing how.** He worked it out himself. When Strahd's slumber opened the borders, he tried the Mists carrying the heirloom, and it pulled him home. He is a scientist who tests things. No teacher, so no witness.
- **Use.** He crossed out during Strahd's slumber and could bring followers who came willingly. That fits the earlier cohorts and the hidden work.
- **Return.** Entering needs no outbound talisman, but a closed border sends a talisman holder to the Wandering table. The simplest fix is that Strahd opened the border for the servants he recalled. Cassian may also have carried a Barovian talisman, such as a bottle of Barovian wine.
- **Fate.** When Strahd woke, the ring went dead. Cassian hid it from Strahd with his research. It can sit inert in the Vallaki strongbox as a clue and a hook (who owned it), not as an exit.
- **Do not reuse** for Grim's concept, which also floats a mist talisman for the coven.

**Open hazards and questions**
- **Wine bottles.** Cassian's wine could itself be a Barovia talisman. If leftover bottles at Widow's Peak work, the neighbors or Milosh could be drawn into Barovia.
- **Entering the Mists.** The sources assume the holder is already "in the Mists". Decide whether using a talisman raises a mist bank near its origin. That would also explain the unnatural mist at the forest edge in Agni's plot hook.
- **Strahd opening the border.** He can open and close it at will, so a talisman could work whenever he chooses. Keep this as his concession to a named creature, not something the party can trigger.
- **Keeper ravens.** *Van Richten's Guide to Ravenloft* gives the Keepers carrier ravens that cross the Mists. Correspondence stays removed, but it exists as a canon channel if you reconsider.

## How It Differs from the Other Players

- **Caspian:** His center is justice for Ashcairn and the werewolf raid, with a wider Shadowfell identity. Shared taken-child stakes should stay incidental to his den story, not become Agni's core.
- **Grim:** Her center is Milo, the hag coven, and the Mists' bargain. Agni also seeks his son, so distinguish them through ongoing treatment and a father's relationship with a son who can communicate and make choices, not a hidden captive-rescue and hag-deal plot.
- **Ivan:** His center is returning to a divided family and clan, leadership, and the Huntress. Agni's family is outside the Mists and his conflict is alchemical and bodily; do not make him another werewolf-family reconciliation arc.
- **Sister Elga:** Her center is the Ven, the trapped dead, and her doctrine. Her brewing with Agni is a meaningful shared activity, but the solution should not make her the owner of Agni's cure or turn her soul work into Agni's main plot.

## Player Preferences (DM Input)

Agni's player likes good lore and difficult battles, and is stubborn. There is still no `player_profile.md`; these are the only recorded preferences.

- **Good lore:** Reveal Osric's origin in layers. Widow's Peak as his birthplace, the heirloom, his notes on the failed cohorts and the three-tier cure are all findable. Let the lore explain the mechanics, so research is the reward.
- **Difficult battles:** Osric's fight in Arc O/P must be hard and not a static HP race. Per the combat skill, give it an objective besides killing him (a ritual, the heart-blood, an escape route) and something to react to (his Pull, phases, retreat). Earlier fights against his creations can escalate the difficulty.
- **Stubborn:** Treat refusal as a real, costly option, not a failed save. Agni's vow against feeding on living blood and his refusal to give up on Milosh are strengths. If he charges Osric early, Strahd's court and Ravenloft's rules should answer with consequences, not a railroad. Give leverage (Defiance marks, notes, Elga's help) so stubbornness pays off.
