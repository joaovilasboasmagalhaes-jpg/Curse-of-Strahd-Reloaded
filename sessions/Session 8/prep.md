### Strahd's conversation topic bank

> Regarding the Section 2 question: “Prepare Strahd’s player-specific conversation points: greetings, questions, and reactions.”

Working topic bank, not scripted dialogue. Strahd can speak from what he has observed or learned, but questions are invitations; he does not know private histories unless a credible source or the characters themselves reveal them. The foretelling makes him especially attentive to the party as newcomers, but he does not disclose it.

One personal topic per player:

- **Agni:** Osric's report informs Strahd's private interest, but Strahd reveals none of it. He may infer from Agni's burgundy eyes and Rahadin's earlier sniff that Agni has an unusual relationship to blood; he should frame this as a question, not claim knowledge of Agni's history or choices. Possible line: *"Your eyes are an uncommon shade, Master Vidar. Does the hunger trouble you?"* A measured pause or closer look can show Strahd's interest without explaining it. Do not mention Osric, the experiment, Milosh, or the cohort.
- **Caspian:** Strahd's spies could have heard Caspian openly express hatred of him in the village and discuss his grievance with Grim at the ruined tower (Sessions 3 and 4). If Caspian mentions werewolves, Strahd gives him his full attention and asks, *"Werewolves? Tell me what happened."* If Caspian says they served Strahd, he replies, *"Quite possibly. But you have yet to tell me whether they carried my orders, or merely spoke my name."* If Caspian describes the raid and demands justice, Strahd asks, *"You seek the creatures responsible. What has persuaded you that I commanded them?"* If Caspian asks him to punish the attackers, he says, *"You ask for my judgment, then. Give me something upon which to judge."* Strahd has no prior knowledge of the specific raid, Ashcairn, its children, or Ravka. He knows Clan Venatricho exists and that Kiril pledged loyalty, but does not know Ivan belongs to the clan or connect Ivan or Kiril to Caspian's raid. Do not have him volunteer Kiril's name in response to Caspian; if Ivan names Kiril, use Ivan's separate topic.
- **Grim:** Strahd has no prior knowledge of Milo or the bargain; let her choose what to disclose. He notices the Living Shadow if it moves independently or appears where the lighting should not produce one, not automatically: *"Your shadow appears to have interests of its own. Has it accompanied you long?"* The shadow alone does not identify its source; her account of the bargain could make him suspect the Dark Powers. He studies her with curiosity rather than treating her as favored or immediately resenting her. The pouch is flesh, not human skin: no Ba'al Verzi association. If he sees the heart, hears its pulse, or is told about it, his own research makes him attentive, but he cannot identify its magic or locate Milo: *"It beats still. What have you been told sustains it?"* If she explains the bond: *"What did they ask in return?"* If she mentions her missing son, he asks his name and what happened, interested in her loss and determination rather than promising a rescue. He does not reveal his own Heart or experiments.
- **Ivan:** Strahd may know Ivan's name from spies and knows from a brief report relayed through Ludmilla that Clan Venatricho exists and Kiril came from the southern woods. He does not know Ivan is a member of the clan or Kiril's brother. Use the ordinary C2 greeting; do not say *"Your line survives"* or imply Wolfir detection. If Ivan identifies himself as Kiril's brother or says Kiril sought Strahd's alliance, Strahd confirms only the narrow fact he knows: *"Kiril pledged his loyalty. I accepted it."* He does not claim Kiril has proved useful or describe their dealings. If Ivan says Kiril threatened the pack in Strahd's name, Strahd answers evenly: *"I did not instruct him to threaten your people. I accepted his pledge; I did not give him leave to speak for my will in every private quarrel."* This is the first time Strahd hears of the threat. His attention sharpens after Eva's reading, so he asks one focused follow-up: *"What, precisely, did he tell you I wanted?"* This is a dialogue proposal, not newly locked campaign canon.
- **Sister Elga:** Rahadin addressed her as a priest of the Ven, so Strahd may address her by her title. Let his gaze linger on the Urn, then say: *"A lantern for lost things. I once knew a great many people who kept such lanterns."* He moves on; if pressed, he declines to explain. The line hints at his past knowledge of the Ven without revealing the Urn's contents or claiming a connection to the Heart.

Topics already in the campaign notes:

- **Introductions and the newcomers:** Strahd knows they came from beyond the Mists and has heard of their exploits from his friends and spies.
- **Death House:** Their actions against its cult and occupants; the nature or purpose of the house; and, if its spirits were put to rest, why he should not punish them for harming his servants.
- **Ismark:** His defiance of Strahd's rule and whether he deserves punishment.
- **Ireena:** Strahd's claim over her, whether he should take her to Ravenloft, and whether he will let her leave the crossroads safely.

- **Freedom and the domain:** Whether Strahd trapped them in Barovia, why he will not release them, and his refusal to grant freedom.
- **Escher:** If asked, Strahd introduces his driver as his coachman and cupbearer.
- **The hidden foretelling:** It is not a conversation topic to reveal. It privately sharpens Strahd's curiosity about the newcomers and his attention to how they act.

Other possible topics:

- What each character seeks in Barovia, and what they intend to do next.
- What they believe makes a ruler just, and whether they consider themselves responsible for the people they protect.
- Who among them makes decisions, and what would make them abandon or defy one another. Keep this as probing curiosity, not an interrogation or a claim that Strahd already knows their loyalties.

Use only topics that arise naturally in the conversation; this is a menu, not a checklist to exhaust.
