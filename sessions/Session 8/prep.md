**Planned beats, in order:**

#### Strahd's Arrival

Why did Strahd go to sleep?

> **Copilot (answered by History of Barovia, "The Decades Pass").** Boredom, then a finished plan. Centuries of confinement left him melancholic, and he already retreated to his coffin for weeks or months at a time until servants woke him. After Madam Eva foretold the Grand Conjunction (over a century away), he built an escape plan around the Heart of Sorrow, binding the Fanes to it. When the plan was complete "he found little else to retain his attention" and was "weary of his subjects". He chose a final hibernation of a hundred years while the Heart grew in power, and told no one, not even Rahadin. So the sleep is a deliberate wait, not weakness. Doru's mob woke him early.

> **Copilot (the files spell it "Zelenna").** A witch of Baba Lysaga's coven during Strahd's sleep, the strongest of the younger members. They resented Lysaga's devotion to a sleeping Strahd. Lugdana and Livius Martikov, with the Holy Symbol of Ravenkind, banished Chernovog, but Lugdana died of her wound and the Symbol was lost. In Arc U Strahd calls her "a mewling witch that believed herself the harbinger of a god". That is why he finds the Forest Fane hard to control in the Arc C foretelling. The files never state what became of her. One tension to watch: the Strahd file says he "moved quickly to reclaim the Forest Fane", while Arc C says its power is still hard to control. #### Tser Pool

> **Copilot (answered).** In the original book most Barovians are soulless, and Strahd cannot feed on them. Reloaded removes that. So no native Barovian is born soulless in this version. Souls can still be lost or taken. Examples: Stella Wachter's soulless body in Arc H, and the Amber vestiges claiming slivers of soul. Players hearing "Barovians have no souls" from a Vistana would be wrong in this campaign. Stanimir should simply not say it.
