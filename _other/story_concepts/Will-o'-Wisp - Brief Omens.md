# Concept: Will-o'-Wisp - Brief Omens

> [!info] Concept file
> Status: `exploring`. These are alternative ideas, not campaign canon. No option has been chosen or applied.

## Idea

Replace C4e's optional will-o'-wisp detour with a brief, mysterious, noncombat encounter. Remove the lure to the ruined tower, chest, and buried zombies from this encounter; this is separate from C6's later watchtower.

The DM wants something shorter that fits the mystical atmosphere of the Tarokka session. It might recall shared history, touch individual memories, foreshadow later events, or simply leave an evocative mystery.

Use one option, not a sequence of them. Target roughly 3-8 minutes of table time, with no required check, combat, treasure, or new quest. The party can decline to engage and continue. Preserve the existing optional trigger (leaving the road to conceal their tracks) unless the DM separately chooses to change it.

## Story beats

### Option 1: Five Windows Home

1. **Setup:** A pale light hangs above a shallow puddle. The puddle reflects neither the trees nor the people looking into it.
2. **Escalation:** Each character sees a different familiar threshold: somewhere they remember from before Barovia. Ask each player privately or openly which doorway, gate, or entrance their character recognizes. Accept an established memory or a player-supplied harmless detail; do not invent a backstory revelation.
3. **Choice:** They may describe what they recognize, share it with the others, touch the water, or walk away. If someone reaches in, the image ripples; there is only shallow water beneath it. This is not a portal.
4. **Payoff:** The reflection returns to ordinary trees. The wisp is gone. Nothing establishes whether these were memories, a lure, or something responding to their longing.

**Connection:** The personal reasons the party shared in Session 8, without another set of individualized prophecies.

**Risk:** Five private scenes could become long. Limit each to one image and one response. Do not show the current condition of missing relatives, reveal secrets to other PCs automatically, or imply that an absent person is dead.

### Option 2: The Silver Wound

1. **Setup:** The wisp passes close to a wet stone. A thin line of frost follows its light.
2. **Escalation:** From one angle the frost is a dragon with a wing missing. When someone moves their light or changes viewpoint, separate patches briefly align into a whole dragon.
3. **Choice:** The players may examine it, sketch it, try to preserve it, or move on. It is an image to notice, not a puzzle with a required solution.
4. **Payoff:** The frost melts. The dragon may later come to mind when they encounter the broken silver pendant at C6 or the Order of the Silver Dragon.

**Connection:** Established silver-dragon imagery and the later pendant. The frost manifestation is proposed.

**Risk:** Do not give the Order's name or history here. The scene must not become a direction to investigate a new ruin. The pendant remains where the campaign places it; no duplicate treasure.

### Option 3: The Road Remembers

1. **Setup:** The wisp shines across an empty patch of mud. Small footprints appear, then larger ones bracketing them.
2. **Escalation:** For a moment its light outlines two adults shielding a child, recalling the Langsten family's protective behavior in Session 7.
3. **Choice:** The party may acknowledge the figures, offer a gesture, discuss the resemblance, or pass in silence. No particular response is demanded or judged.
4. **Payoff:** The outlines fade, leaving the party's own footprints crossing the older impressions. The wisp moves away.

**Connection:** A shared act of mercy that actually happened. This lets the session look backward before Eva looks forward.

**Risk:** Elga already gathered the Langstens' souls into her urn. These must be apparent echoes of the event, not their souls trapped outside it again. Do not declare the phenomenon an expression of their gratitude or establish new soul mechanics.

### Option 4: The Unchosen Shadows

1. **Setup:** The wisp passes between the party and a bank of pale mist. Their silhouettes appear upon it.
2. **Escalation:** The silhouettes show each character standing alone, separated by much greater distances than the real party. No silhouette performs an action on the character's behalf.
3. **Choice:** Characters can approach one another, reach toward the mist, question the sight, or ignore it. If they bring their lights together, the separate silhouettes overlap into an ordinary group shadow.
4. **Payoff:** The mist breaks apart and the wisp vanishes. Whether they saw a possible future, an expression of fear, or an optical impossibility remains unanswered.

**Connection:** The party's still-forming common purpose after Session 8. The image poses a question about staying together rather than supplying another prophecy.

**Risk:** Do not call this fate or imply that cooperation is mechanically compulsory. The upcoming March of the Dead already warns about failure; keep this about isolation, not another vision of their deaths.

### Option 5: A Place at the Fire

1. **Setup:** The wisp hovers beside a cold, abandoned campfire ring just off their path. Its light falls on the empty place where someone might once have sat.
2. **Escalation:** If a character sits there or tends the embers, a second, smaller point of light appears opposite them. An otherwise empty patch of grass briefly bends as though under someone's weight.
3. **Choice:** They may offer company, speak without expecting an answer, attempt to kindle the fire, or leave. There is no correct offering and no toll.
4. **Payoff:** After a few quiet moments, both lights disappear. If the party lit an ordinary fire, it remains until they extinguish it; otherwise the ring stays cold. No person, reward, or request appears.

**Connection:** A quiet contrast to the crowded Dancing Fire: companionship offered without knowing who receives it.

**Risk:** This is the least explanatory option. Do not turn the empty seat into another mystery quest, promise that a soul was released, or require lengthy improvised dialogue. The physical campfire ring is a proposed minor feature, not an established landmark.

### Option 6: The Missing Moment

The DM's idea: a short sequence that feels real and dangerous, interrupted by an abrupt, unannounced cut to the crossroads. The players, not only their characters, experience the break in continuity.

1. **Placement:** If the party chooses the existing off-road route, put the encounter at its end, within sight of the crossroads. The crossing is a few steps away on safe ground, not a thirty-minute journey skipped while the characters are unable to act. If they do not go off-road, omit it unless the DM changes the trigger.
2. **Setup:** The real will-o'-wisp glows in the trees, up to 100 feet away. **Elga's Urn glows: tell the player.** The wisp is not a guide or an identified villain; characters may approach it, ignore it, or act against it.
3. **Escalation:** A proposed local haunting briefly overlays the characters' surroundings. Use the ravine and apparent landslide below to make immediate escape appealing, while letting the characters decide their own actions. The actual area has no such falling hazard.
4. **Cut:** For the brief version, at the first decisive action describe the real surroundings without a transition. For the chase alternative below, let the chase play out first. If they reach the road, it can be the crossroads: the gravel beneath their feet, the gallows, and Eva waiting. If they stay, split up, or turn back, cut to their actual locations instead, with the crossroads nearby. Do not teleport or reposition characters to force the planned cut.
5. **Aftermath:** They remember the apparent danger and their own choices. The landslide is absent; the light's location and the urn's glow reflect the real wisp's position. Give the table a moment to react before continuing toward Eva. Whether they understand the phenomenon is their choice.

**Suggested table delivery:** When a player asks about the landslide, preserve the discontinuity without denying what the player heard: "You remember the cracking wood clearly. There's no fallen debris here." Let the players compare notes; if someone is genuinely confused about the game state or asks out of character, explain promptly that they experienced a strange perception and clarify their actual positions. Keep its cause mysterious.

**Actual versus apparent:** Characters physically move according to their declared actions on the real terrain; they do not sleep, teleport, or lose time. Their conversations and real actions happened. Spells, ammunition, and other resources used are really spent, and normal outcomes of real actions remain. The phantom landslide deals no damage because it does not physically occur; it is not an attack roll or a saving throw. If someone attacks the actual wisp, resolve the attack and its consequences normally; it may flee or the encounter may end early. Do not rewind resources selectively, decide how PCs reacted, or cancel their achievements to guarantee the twist.

**Separate real circumstances:** The encounter does not decide whether Eliza followed them or remove her. Resolve the existing tracking choices normally, and establish her actual distance. If the party separated, do not reunite them by narration. Ireena and other companions remain wherever their real movement places them. The perceived threat is the environment, not an invented creature; Eliza may still be present if the party failed to lose her.

**Connection:** The shared reading is next, so uncertainty about what can be seen and trusted sets the tone without providing another prophecy. The broken memory can prompt the party to talk about what each perceived.

**Risk:** A prolonged denial of what was actually said can break trust rather than create mystery. Make the cut the trick, not a prolonged dispute with players. If a player wants an out-of-character clarification or seems uncomfortable, pause and clarify immediately. Do not equate this magical event with an actual psychiatric condition.

**Rules boundary:** This is a proposed shared supernatural perception, not an existing will-o'-wisp power or a claim that the characters have a medical condition. It does not impose a new condition, force actions, or override senses and class features. An active Blood Sense reports real living creatures within 30 feet as normal. If a spell or feature would interact with the phenomenon, adjudicate its actual text rather than announcing that it automatically fails; the exact magic and applicable countermeasures remain open for design.

#### A possible vision: The Pack Below

The next likely combat threat on the westward route is the wolves at C7a's ravine. They stalk the party, close from ahead and behind, and try to knock one traveler over the edge; two more wait below. Later, C11's wolves and werewolves try to isolate and drag away a party member. This vision foreshadows the **tactic**, not an inevitable outcome or the identity of every wolf.

The real wisp hovers about 80 feet away in the trees. The party is on safe ground, close to the crossroads. A proposed local haunting briefly makes the terrain seem to change. The DM wants the apparent danger to make the party want to run immediately, not to fight or negotiate:

> The light flickers between the trees. Ahead, the ground seems to fall away into a deep, mist-filled ravine.
>
> From below comes a low growl. Two pairs of amber eyes are watching from the rocks.
>
> A crack splits the air above you. A boulder tears through the trees and crashes onto the ground nearby. More follow. The slope is coming down—trees, earth and stone rushing toward you. A gap through the trees leads away from it.

Pause for an immediate response. The apparent hazard is the oncoming landslide, not a creature to fight; the eyes are a brief clue rather than a confrontation. The wolves, ravine, and landslide are appearances, not actual wolves, a cliff, or falling debris here; what the party does is real. The DM chooses the timing and delivery of the cut.

**Opportunity:** Fleeing together is the obvious response, but characters may attempt other actions. The scene cannot guarantee a particular player choice and does not magically compel flight. When C7a later presents the actual ravine, eyes below can be a useful warning rather than merely decorative. The later encounter does not establish a landslide; it is dream imagery, not a prediction of one.

**Continuity and caution:** It echoes Ivan's documented pursuit and Caspian's search without revealing Ivan's nature or asserting that these wolves attacked Caspian's village. It does not reveal the later werewolves' identities or guarantee that the same events occur. The real wisp can be targeted; don't substitute an illusion for an attack on it.

> [!warning] Agni's Blood Sense
> **Agni's Blood Sense: check thirst stage/activation; tell the player if active.** The apparent wolves remain at least 40 feet away while the vision lasts and are not living creatures. The actual undead wisp is also not detected. Report all real living creatures within 30 feet, including companions and Eliza if she is following close enough, giving their actual positions, bloodied status, and which has the lowest current HP. If the party moves, check when real creatures enter or leave range. Never assert the woods are empty if an actual creature is present.

> [!warning] Elga's Urn
> **Elga's Urn glows: tell the player** when the real wisp comes within 300 feet. The vision's wolves do not cause the glow. Other actual undead may also keep it glowing.

**Alternative, later payoff:** At St. Andral's Church, the stolen bones leave the sanctuary at risk from Volenta and her vampire spawn. A brief image of a candlelit refuge whose light is suddenly extinguished could foreshadow the danger, but not a fixed attack: the party can recover the bones and prevent it. The wolf image is a stronger immediate tactical warning and avoids revealing the church's investigation in advance.

#### Chase alternative: The Slope That Follows

The DM proposed a short pursuit with the party as quarry. This is the existing optional off-road will-o'-wisp encounter, not an additional scene. It and the chase after Eliza are **mutually exclusive**: run at most one chase, whichever the party's choices and circumstances bring about. Either can introduce the chase rules; neither is guaranteed to occur.

- **Off-road to conceal tracks:** The wisp encounter can become the party-as-quarry chase. Do not also run an Eliza chase afterward.
- **Eliza flees and the party chases her:** Run her chase instead. If the party later goes off-road to conceal tracks, the wisp encounter may still happen, but use a non-chase version or omit it.
- **Neither chase occurs:** Continue normally. The party is not required to flee the apparent hazard or pursue Eliza.

Going off-road does not automatically mean Eliza loses them: resolve the existing concealment checks and other actions normally. If she still follows and Eva spots her after the wisp chase, preserve her presence and the consequences of the party's choices, but do not stage a second chase. How to resolve any attempted pursuit briefly without a second chase remains open.

The wisp appears along the existing off-road route, at a proposed distance of roughly 150-200 feet from the crossroads along a real, passable course; that precise placement is not established by the campaign. It is within 300 feet of Elga. The characters may engage with or ignore it. The apparent ravine and wolves appear as above. Then the hillside seems to collapse and keep coming after them, filling the woods with falling timber, earth, and stone. The pursuing landslide, ravine, and wolves are unreal; the actual area has a real, traversable course.

> The light wavers beyond the trees. To one side the ground falls away, and two pairs of amber eyes watch from far below.
>
> A crack snaps overhead. A boulder crashes through the branches. Behind it, the hillside begins to move: earth, stones, and trees rolling toward you. There is a gap through the trees ahead.
>
> The first stones strike the ground behind you. The rumble follows.

If the party chooses to flee, use [Dungeon Master's Guide, Chases](../../assets/Dungeon%20Master's%20Guide.md#chases), with these **proposed adaptations** for an illusory environmental pursuer:

- The moving wall begins 60 feet behind the nearest character. Roll initiative for participants and for the pursuing wall; track the distances. The wall is an apparent hazard, not a creature or a normal published chase participant. As a simple DM-controlled proxy, it advances 60 feet on each of its turns; it makes no attacks. If it reaches a character, the appearance ends for the group before impact. An attack or spell that could stop or expose the apparent threat is adjudicated on its actual terms; do not make the wall automatically immune just to preserve the chase.
- Use a couple of **real** features of the actual course, such as a low branch and muddy brush, as mapped obstacles. Apply an appropriate published Wilderness Chase Complications result to an obstacle if appropriate, rather than silently pretending an illusory obstruction caused a physical injury. If using the random table instead, each participant rolls at the end of their turn, and the result affects the next participant in initiative order.
- Run ordinary movement and actions. Characters can Dash, assist each other, split up, or try something else; all physical movement, resources spent, and consequences of real obstacles persist. Characters who escape are no longer pursued; let others continue if separated. A quarry can test escape at the end of each round using the published Stealth procedure once out of the lead pursuer's sight; the apparent wall uses a **proposed passive Perception of 12** solely for this chase procedure. A successful escape, stopping, reaching the crossroads, or the wall catching someone can end the chase for that character as applicable. The illusion ends for everyone when the wall catches someone or all characters have escaped or otherwise stopped participating.
- The DM handles the abrupt transition. Place everyone where their actual decisions and movement leave them, even if they split up or do not reach the crossroads. A character who stops or confronts the apparent wall does not have to run. The real wisp is a separate creature and can be targeted normally.

**Tutorial value and limit:** The chase demonstrates initiative, movement, action choices, a complication, tracked distance, and possibly escape checks. A two- or three-round chase may not reach the Dash/exhaustion threshold, so do not claim that it teaches that rule unless it actually comes up. At the end of a round, a quarry never out of the lead pursuer's sight automatically fails its escape check. Once it is out of sight, compare its Dexterity (Stealth) check to pursuers' passive Perception as in the DMG.

**Agni:** The apparent wall is not a living creature, nor are the distant wolf images; the wisp is undead. The actual living companions and any other living creature, including Eliza if within 30 feet, are detected normally when Blood Sense is active. Track their real distances as they move; do not conceal a qualifying creature to protect the reveal.

**Elga:** **Elga's Urn glows: tell the player** while the real wisp is within 300 feet, including if invisible.

**Unresolved:** This is a homebrew way to apply the chase framework to an apparent environmental threat; the DMG requires a pursuer and does not provide a landslide stat block. Decide the phenomenon's nature and how relevant features or spells interact before running it. It may take longer than the original 3-8-minute goal with five PCs and companions.
